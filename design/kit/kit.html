Core and spread

One element per screen that everything else comes out of and goes back into. Nothing teleports. On this page it is the puck in the bottom corner.

Toasts leave the core and stack: the front one is readable, up to three wait behind it. It never shows more than four. A fifth makes the front one pull in to a bead and drop back into the core. Each front toast stays five seconds; hover to hold it. Its close button on the right sends it back into the core early, with the same drop.

Rail: the desktop form of the same core

Press the menu icon in the rail.

56px closed, wider open, on the spread curve. Labels arrive a beat after the width settles, nearest first. The current section sits on highlighter.

The liquid layer

Drops leave the body and rejoin it with a visible neck between them. This layer is decoration only: it sits behind real controls and is hidden from assistive technology. It is a look, not a simulation: nothing has to keep its volume.

Where liquid shows up

  • The puck wobbles once when you reach for it and when a toast leaves or rejoins it.
  • Toasts rise out of it as a bead and widen; the front one drops back in as a bead.
  • Buttons squash under the press and spring back; a follow-up pulls the button in to a wobbling bead.
  • Switches and the speed control stretch a bead across to the new position before letting go of the old one.
  • Progress is led by a bead that pulls the fill after it.
  • Cards opening into a dialog go soft at the corners in flight and firm up on arrival.
  • A sent message, a new list item, a tag, a checked-off task each travel to where they belong as a bead and widen into the finished thing.
  • Follow sends a bead to the puck, which keeps count.
  • The ceremony word separates out of a bead of ink.

Hand-off: what you typed becomes the message

Type something and send it. The words in the field melt into a bead of ink, the bead travels into the thread, and it widens into the bubble with your text on it. One body the whole way: nothing is deleted in one place and created in another. The replies are canned examples.

    Motion

    Two channels. Position, size and shape move on springs and may overshoot. Colour and opacity never bounce. The more often something happens, the less it moves.

    Playback speed
    How often it happensMotion allowed
    Many times a minute: typing, shortcuts, moving through a listNone. The state changes instantly.
    Many times a session: open the menu, toggle, selectFeedback and fade. A spread only for the core.
    A few times a session: open a record, finish a taskSpread and gather, staged.
    Once: first run, a result, a milestoneCeremony.

    Resting controls

    Buttons, fields and tables rest on paper. They respond in 100ms but do not travel. If everything moves, the core stops meaning anything.

    Any button can take a follow-up: after the press it pulls in to an icon for what it just did, the icon makes one small movement, and the button comes back. Follow comes back as Unfollow. A destructive button plays its follow-up only once the deletion has really happened, never in place of a confirmation.

    Easter eggs

    Every app hides at least one thing. There is one on this page. The clue: mercury does not like being poked.

    The contract

    • Off the critical path. Nothing is reachable only through an egg.
    • Earned or opt-in, and free to ignore.
    • Rare and varied.
    • Never in an error, payment, security or data-loss moment.
    • Keyboard reachable, still under reduced motion, silent.
    • Off with the Playfulness switch.
    • Logged in the project's EGGS.md.
